OmniStack, explained
like a human.

No jargon wall. Just what it is, what it does for you, and why it feels different from every AI chat box you've used before.

So… what is OmniStack?

Most AI apps are a text box. You type, it answers, and the moment you close the tab the whole thing evaporates. OmniStack is different: it's more than a harness, it's an agentic platform. Think of it as a capable crewmate that sits inside your computer. You hand it a job — a question, a repo to fix, a deck to build, a process to automate — and it actually does the job: reading your files, using tools, making things, and showing you the receipts along the way.

It runs on your desktop (with a companion app for your phone), it works with the AI models you choose to connect, and it never touches anything sensitive without asking first.

The chat is just the front door

Sure, you can chat with it — quick questions, small tasks, "explain this error message." That's the Interactive mode, and it's fast and friendly. But behind that front door there's a whole building:

  • Coding mode — point it at a project and it will look around, make changes, run the tests and check its own work before claiming victory.
  • Research mode — it gathers current sources, compares them, and tells you honestly what it couldn't confirm.
  • Planner mode — big, sprawling goals get broken into a queue of small pieces, finished one bounded stage at a time.
  • Monitoring mode — it can re-check something on a schedule and ping you when things meaningfully change.
  • Computer use — when there's no better way, it can look at your screen and, with your explicit go-ahead, use the mouse and keyboard. It always looks again after acting.

One objective, eight ways to execute it. You describe what you want; OmniStack picks how to get there — and shows its working.

Missions: the thing you'll fall in love with

A Mission is a job that has to actually finish. Instead of babysitting a chat with "ok, now do the next part," you describe the outcome once: "Get the test suite green, don't change the public API, show me proof."

OmniStack then works through it with a real plan. It saves its progress constantly, keeps a permanent diary of everything it did, and attaches evidence — test output, screenshots, files it changed. If it needs a decision from you, it pauses politely and waits. If your laptop dies mid-run, the Mission picks up exactly where it left off. No lost work, no amnesia, no pretending.

Long jobs have a budget of 500 steps. If a Mission reaches it, OmniStack doesn't silently quit — it asks you, plainly, whether to keep going. You're always the one who decides.

When a Mission finishes, you can open a normal follow-up chat right from its result — with the outcome and evidence already there — so "great, now tweak one thing" never means starting over.

Agents: specialists that remember

You know how you'd explain your coding standards or your brand voice differently to different colleagues? OmniStack lets you create Agents — named specialists with their own personality, instructions and memory. Make a "Release Engineer" that's fussy about verification, a "Researcher" that always cites sources, a "Brand Designer" that knows your palette by heart.

They live in the model picker, one click away. Their memory carries across every job you give them, so you describe your standards once — not every single time.

It can build its own tools (and yours)

This is the quietly wild part. If OmniStack needs a tool that doesn't exist, you can ask it to build one. These Agent apps can talk to APIs, keep secrets encrypted, work with files you approve and run sandboxed commands. Build a stock-price watcher, a release checklist runner, a little dashboard — once made, they stick around for every future conversation.

It can also turn your playbooks into reusable Skills with slash commands ("turn this checklist into /release-audit"), and connect to external services through MCP servers — all from plain conversation.

Automations you can actually see

Repeatable process? Describe it once and OmniStack draws it as a flowchart — real boxes, real decision diamonds, real success checks — that you can read at a glance and run whenever you like. These Workflows run on the same durable engine as Missions, so they get the same budgets, approvals, pause-and-resume superpowers.

The creative side

  • Design systems — describe a mood or show it a reference image, and it generates a real design-token stylesheet with a live component gallery. Your decks and pages can reuse the same visual language.
  • Presentations — give it a subject, review the researched outline, approve, and get a styled slide deck you can present fullscreen or export to PowerPoint.
  • Visualizations — ask it to show you something — a simulation, a comparison, a state machine — and you get an interactive piece with sliders and play buttons, not a wall of text.

Everything it makes lands in the artifact bar beside your conversation: code, reports, images, decks, tools. Preview them, reopen them, download them. The chat stays a chat; the good stuff stays close.

Your stuff stays yours

OmniStack is deliberately not a walled garden. You bring your own AI providers and models (and can switch per conversation). You bring your files, your accounts, your rules. Powerful abilities — network access, running commands, using secrets, controlling the screen — are each separately gated, clearly explained, and entirely your call. Grants are visible and revocable at any time. An organization can also lock down what's allowed, and the enforcement happens in the app itself, not just by hiding buttons.

Desktop brain, pocket companion

The desktop app is where the heavy lifting happens, and the Android companion keeps you in the loop: check on a running Mission, approve a waiting question, get a push alert when something finishes or changes. Start a job at your desk, keep an eye on it from the couch.

The one-paragraph version

OmniStack turns the chat box into a command center. Pick any connected model or one of your specialist Agents. Ask a question, change a repository, create a design system, build a presentation, or launch a durable Mission. It picks a strategy, uses only the powers you approved, records evidence as it goes, and pauses cleanly when it needs you. Chat is where AI talks. OmniStack is where it works.
